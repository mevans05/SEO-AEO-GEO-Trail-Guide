# Outreach playbook

How to convert the tiered target list in §3 into placements. The list tells you where to
go; this tells you what to send and in what order.

## The asset comes first

Outreach without something worth linking to fails on reply rate, and no amount of
sequencing fixes it. Before the first email goes out, one of these has to exist:

1. **Proprietary data.** A survey, a cut of product data, an index. The strongest asset
   class by a distance, and the only one that earns *repeat* citations: a statistic gets
   quoted for years by people who never spoke to you, and LLMs surface quotable figures
   with attribution because that is what the surrounding text does.
2. **A genuinely better reference page.** The definitive explainer on a narrow topic
   where existing coverage is thin.
3. **An expert with a real point of view.** A named person who can be quoted saying
   something a competitor would not say.
4. **A free tool or calculator.** Expensive to build, durable once built.

If none exists, the first month of the plan builds one. Say that in the document rather
than scheduling outreach that will not convert. A common and defensible §3 opening is:
weeks 1–3 build the asset, weeks 4–12 run outreach against it.

## Motion by tier

### T1 — Citation gap

The highest-leverage list. These domains are already being read by the engines for your
prompts; they just do not mention you.

Before pitching, **read the specific page that was cited**. `analysis.json` carries
`top_cited_url` for each target. That page tells you what the engine pulled, and
therefore what a placement needs to look like to get pulled too. Pitching a domain
without reading the page that made it a target is the most common way this list gets
wasted.

Three angles, in order of conversion rate:

1. **The omission.** Their piece covers the category and does not include you. Ask to be
   added, and supply the row — the comparison line, the pricing, the differentiator —
   so inclusion costs them five minutes.
2. **The correction.** Their piece contains something outdated about you or the
   category. Lead with the correction, supply the accurate version. High conversion,
   because it is a favour to them.
3. **The addition.** Their piece is good and missing a dimension you can supply with
   original data.

Target ~15–20% reply rate and ~5–8% placement rate on a well-researched T1 list. Below
that, the asset is the problem, not the list.

### T2 — Competitive displacement

They cover rivals and not you. The piece already exists in the format you need, which
makes these the fastest conversions in the document.

The pitch writes itself: *"You covered [rivals] in [piece]. You did not include us, and
here is the specific reason your readers would want us in there — [differentiator that
is true and checkable]."*

Be honest about fit. If you genuinely do not belong in a roundup, pitch a different
piece rather than arguing your way into one; a bad-faith inclusion request costs the
relationship for the next three.

### T3 — Authority build

No relationship yet. Do not cold-pitch these — route them to PR (§4) or partnerships
(§5). What works is a months-long motion: comment on their reporting, supply data for a
story that is not about you, offer an executive as a source, meet them at an event.
Budget two to three quarters and track relationship stage rather than links.

### HELD — Already linking

Cheapest wins in the document, and routinely skipped. Check every held placement for
accuracy: a stale claim in a frequently-cited source propagates into AI answers long
after you have fixed your own site. Ask for refreshes where the piece is dated, and
deepen the relationship where the outlet is high-frequency.

## Motion by source archetype

The archetype column in §3 exists because an email is the wrong tool for most of the
list.

| Archetype | What actually works | Owner | Typical lag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Editorial / trade | Pitch with an asset | Content / SEO | 2–8 weeks |
| Tier-1 news | Story, spokesperson, embargo — never a link request | PR | 1–6 months |
| Review platform | Review-velocity campaign, profile completeness, category placement | Customer success | 1–3 months |
| Community | Participate as a named expert, disclose affiliation | Product marketing | Ongoing |
| Database / directory | Claim, complete, correct the listing | Ops / marketing | 1–2 weeks |
| Encyclopedia | Notability evidence via third-party coverage; never edit your own entry | PR | 6–12 months |
| Awards body | Entry, deadline, fee | PR / brand | Per cycle |
| Institutional | Cite *them*; contribute to consultations and industry data | Policy / exec | 6–12 months |

Two of these carry real risk if handled wrong. **Communities**: Reddit and Quora are
heavily cited by answer engines, and both punish undisclosed promotion severely — an
account that gets banned takes its citations with it. Participate as a named employee
answering questions you are actually qualified to answer. **Encyclopedia**: editing your
own Wikipedia entry is against policy and reliably backfires. Notability is earned
through independent coverage, which is what §4 produces; the entry follows.

## Constructing a pitch

Five parts, in this order:

1. **Evidence you read their work.** Reference the specific cited page. One sentence.
2. **The gap.** What their piece is missing, stated as their readers' problem.
3. **What you supply.** The data, quote, or row — concrete, attached or inline.
4. **Why you.** The credential that makes you the right source.
5. **A low-friction ask.** "Add this row" converts; "would you consider a guest post"
   does not.

Under 150 words. Subject line names the specific piece, not your company.

Do not send the same pitch to twenty domains. The target list is short *because* it is
researched; treating it as a mail-merge list discards the work that produced it.

## Sequencing a quarter

A realistic shape for one dedicated person, adaptable to the team's actual capacity:

**Weeks 1–3.** Build or finish the linkable asset. Claim and correct every database and
directory listing — these are near-instant wins that need no reply from anyone. Audit
HELD placements for stale claims.

**Weeks 4–8.** T2 outreach first: fastest conversion, and early wins fund the patience
T1 needs. Then T1 in batches of 10–15 with genuinely individual research. Launch the
review-velocity campaign if review platforms appear in the graph.

**Weeks 9–12.** Follow-ups (one, after 5–7 days, adding something new — never "just
bumping this"). Open T3 relationships without asking for anything. Enter any award whose
deadline falls in the window. Re-export citation data and measure.

Do not run more than 15–20 genuinely personalised pitches a week per person. Past that,
research quality collapses and reply rate follows it down.

## What to measure

| Metric | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Reply rate | 15–20% | Below this, the asset or the research is weak |
| Placement rate | 5–8% of sent | The honest conversion number |
| T1 capture | +10pp/quarter | The metric that matters: share of cited domains that now link to you |
| Answer share | Re-measure monthly | The outcome; lags placements by 4–12 weeks |
| Time to first citation | Track it | How long a placement takes to appear in an answer |

That last one is worth instrumenting deliberately, because it sets expectations for
everyone funding the work. Engines re-crawl and re-index on their own schedules, so a
placement in week 4 may not show up in an answer until week 12. Teams that do not know
this conclude the programme failed right before it starts working.

## Tactics to refuse

Recommend none of the following, and say so plainly if a client asks:

- **Paid links, link exchanges, PBNs.** Against Google's guidelines, and increasingly
  detectable. The downside is a manual action.
- **Undisclosed sponsored placement.** An FTC problem as well as a search one.
- **Astroturfing reviews or community posts.** Platform bans are permanent and take
  your existing citations with them.
- **Mass-generated guest posts.** Low-quality, and the footprint is obvious.
- **Editing your own Wikipedia entry.** Policy violation, reliably backfires.

These are not cautious hedges — they are the tactics that put a brand's existing
authority at risk, which is exactly the asset this document exists to build. If a
client is already doing one of them, flag it in §4 as something to stop.
