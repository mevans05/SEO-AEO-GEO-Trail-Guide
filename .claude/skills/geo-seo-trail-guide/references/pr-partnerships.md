## Why PR belongs in a GEO document

Answer engines synthesise from text they retrieve. What gets retrieved for a commercial
prompt is overwhelmingly third-party: review platforms, roundups, trade coverage,
community threads. A brand's own site is one voice among those, and usually a discounted
one.

This inverts a familiar priority. For classic SEO, PR is a link-acquisition channel and
the link is the point. For GEO, the *text* is the point — a mention with no link still
puts your name and your claims into the retrieval corpus. That changes what to ask for
and how to measure it. An unlinked mention in a frequently-cited trade title can move
answer share more than a dofollow link from a site no engine has ever cited.

It also changes what "good coverage" means. A profile piece about your funding round is
brand PR. A quoted statistic in a piece about *the category* is GEO PR, because
category pieces are what engines retrieve when someone asks a category question. Steer
the programme toward the second.

## PR process changes

§4 asks for process changes, not just campaigns, because campaigns end and process
compounds. Ground each recommendation in the dataset, and name at least one thing that
should **stop** — a plan that only adds work will not be executed by a team already
fully committed.

**Reactive commentary.** Put a named expert on the journalist-request platforms
(Qwoted, Featured, Source of Sources, Help a B2B Writer) with a standing commitment to
respond inside two hours. Speed wins these outright. This is the cheapest recurring
source of tier-1 mentions available, and most brands staff it inconsistently, which is
why it keeps working.

**Original data as the default.** Shift the programme from announcement-led to
data-led. A quarterly index or benchmark from product data gets cited for years; a
funding announcement gets one news cycle. If §2's source graph shows engines citing
statistics-heavy pages, this is the highest-return process change available.

**Kill the release-only motion.** Wire-service syndication produces duplicate,
nofollowed placements that rarely surface in answers. Releases are for announcements of
record. Coverage comes from pitching, and the wire is not a substitute.

**Consistent expert bylines.** One or two named spokespeople, not a rotating cast.
Entity recognition rewards concentration: engines build a stronger association with a
person who appears consistently across outlets than with five people appearing once each.

**Track mentions, not just links.** Most PR reporting counts links, which means it is
blind to half of GEO value. Add unlinked brand mentions in cited domains to the report.

**Feed PR from the citation data.** The §2 source graph is a media list built from
evidence of what engines actually read. Most PR target lists are built from circulation
figures and habit. Replacing one with the other is a process change worth stating
explicitly.

## Campaign patterns

Ranked by durability of the citations they produce.

**Proprietary data study.** Survey your market or cut your own product data into an
annual index. Produces a quotable statistic — the single most citation-durable asset
that exists, because it gets requoted by people who never contacted you, and each
requote is another retrievable mention. Give it a stable name, a canonical URL, and
publish it annually.

**Category definition.** Name and define a problem or method, then defend the
definition. Slow, and the payoff is that engines answering "what is X" cite the
definer.

**Contrarian expert position.** A named person arguing something specific against the
category's consensus. Earns commentary requests and makes the person a go-to source.

**Newsjacking.** Fast commentary on a category event. Requires pre-agreed positions and
a spokesperson who can be quoted within hours.

**Collaborative research.** Joint study with a partner, association or academic
institution. Doubles distribution and borrows their authority; institutional co-authors
are cited disproportionately.

For each concept in §4, specify: the hook, the asset behind it, named target
publications drawn from §2's table, the spokesperson, and a timeline. A concept that
could run for any brand in any category is not a concept.

## Entity building

Engines need to resolve the brand as a known entity before they will confidently name
it. This is unglamorous, largely one-off, and blocks everything else.

- **Wikidata** entry, with correct properties. Achievable without the notability bar
  Wikipedia sets, and directly consumed by engines.
- **Wikipedia**, if genuinely notable. Requires sustained independent coverage — an
  output of §4, not an input. Never edit your own entry.
- **Crunchbase, LinkedIn, G2, Clutch** profiles complete and mutually consistent.
  Contradictory founding dates or employee counts across sources weaken entity
  confidence.
- **`sameAs` links** in Organization schema pointing at every profile above.
- **Consistent naming.** Pick one form of the brand name and use it everywhere.
- **Named executives** with consistent bios, `Person` schema, and author attribution on
  everything they write.

## Review platforms

For commercial prompts — "best X", "X alternatives", "X vs Y" — review platforms are
frequently the most-cited source type in the whole graph. §2's archetype mix tells you
whether that is true for this brand's prompt set. When it is, this is the highest-ROI
section in the document, and it is not an SEO project.

**Presence.** Claimed, complete profiles on every platform appearing in §2. Correct
category placement matters as much as the profile itself: engines pull category
leaders, so being in the wrong category is invisible regardless of rating.

**Volume and recency.** Both are weighted by the platforms and both decay. A systematic
review-request motion — triggered at a genuine success moment in the customer
lifecycle — beats a periodic push. Never gate on positive sentiment or offer
compensation for a rating; both violate platform terms and G2 and Capterra both police it.

**Comparison pages.** Platforms auto-generate "X vs Y" pages that rank and get cited
heavily. Being present and well-rated on the right comparisons is a direct GEO lever.

**Negative review response.** Engines summarise sentiment. An unanswered critical review
is a cited liability; a well-handled response reads as competence.

This runs through customer success, not marketing. Say so in the document, with an owner.

## Listicle inclusion

"Best X" roundups are what an engine retrieves for the highest-intent commercial
prompts in the set. Getting into them is a specific motion:

1. Pull every roundup ranking or cited for your commercial prompts (§2 and §6 both
   surface these).
2. Split into those including you and those not.
3. For the omissions, pitch inclusion with the row pre-written — the comparison line,
   pricing, the differentiator. Make inclusion a five-minute edit.
4. For the inclusions, check accuracy and freshness. A stale entry in a frequently
   cited roundup misinforms every answer that draws on it.
5. Track which roundups engines actually cite, not which rank highest. They differ more
   than people expect.

## Co-marketing partnerships

- **Integration and marketplace listings.** Partner directories are structured,
  high-authority and frequently cited. Cheap, durable, usually under-exploited.
- **Joint research.** Split the cost, double the distribution, borrow authority.
- **Customer stories** published on both sides.
- **Industry associations.** Membership directories are trusted institutional sources;
  committee participation earns speaking and citation opportunities.
- **Complementary vendor content.** Co-authored guides with non-competing vendors
  serving the same buyer.
- **Event and podcast circuits.** Transcripts are indexed and increasingly retrieved.
  Ask the host for a transcript if they do not publish one.

Choose partners by whether they already appear in §2's citation graph. A partner the
engines already read amplifies into the corpus you are trying to enter; one they do not
is a brand play, which may be worth doing for other reasons but should not be counted
here.

## Awards

Awards earn a high-authority dofollow link from the awarding body, but the GEO reason is
better: winner lists are exactly the pages engines retrieve for "best X",
"award-winning X" and "top X companies" prompts. An award is a durable third-party
credential inserted into the retrieval corpus, and unlike outreach it runs on a
published calendar you can plan a year around.

**Verify every deadline, fee and category before putting it in the document.** These
change annually and a calendar with a stale date is worse than no calendar. The table
below is a starting shortlist and a set of typical timing windows, not a source of truth.

| Programme | Fits | Typical entry window |
|---|---|---|
| Webby Awards | Digital product, site, campaign, video | Entries autumn–winter, winners spring |
| Fast Company World Changing Ideas | Social/environmental impact | Entries winter, winners spring |
| Fast Company Innovation by Design | Design-led product | Entries winter–spring |
| Inc. 5000 | US revenue growth | Entries spring, list autumn |
| Deloitte Technology Fast 500 | Tech revenue growth | Entries summer, list autumn |
| Stevie Awards (multiple programmes) | Business, sales, customer service | Several cycles per year |
| Shorty Awards | Social and digital campaigns | Entries winter |
| Anthem Awards | Purpose and mission work | Entries summer–autumn |
| Fastest-growing / best-workplace regional lists | Local authority, recruiting | Varies by market |
| Category analyst grids (G2, Gartner, Forrester) | Product category standing | Rolling / quarterly |
| Trade association awards | Vertical credibility | Varies |

Selection criteria, in order:

1. **Does winning produce a public, indexable winner page with a link?** Some do not.
   Check before paying.
2. **Does the award name match a prompt in the set?** An award whose name matches
   "best [category] software" is worth several whose names match nothing.
3. **Is the category winnable?** A narrow category you can win beats a flagship you
   cannot. Most programmes have many categories; read the list properly.
4. **Does the fee fit the return?** Entry fees run from modest to four figures per
   category. Note fees in the document — this is a budget line.
5. **Is the programme credible?** Pay-to-win schemes that award everyone produce links
   from low-trust domains and can embarrass the brand. If you have not heard of it and
   it guarantees a win, skip it.

Build the §5 calendar with: programme, category, deadline, fee, owner, and the prompt or
topic a win would support. Sequence entries by deadline, and start the ones needing
customer references or case-study material at least six weeks out.

## Technical prerequisites

None of the above works if engines cannot retrieve or parse the brand's content. Check
these early — they are cheap, and a blocked crawler silently nullifies the entire plan.

**Crawler access.** Confirm `robots.txt` does not block `GPTBot`, `OAI-SearchBot`,
`ClaudeBot`, `PerplexityBot`, `Google-Extended`, `CCBot`. This is the single most
common own-goal in GEO: a blanket block added years ago, or inherited from a template,
removes the brand from the corpus entirely. Check it before anything else, and if it is
blocking, that is the first line of the executive summary.

**Structured data.** `Organization` with `sameAs`, `Product`, `FAQPage` where genuinely
applicable, `Article` with `author`. Schema does not force a citation, but it removes
ambiguity about what the page is and who wrote it.

**Answer-ready formatting.** Question-shaped headings with a direct 40–60 word answer
immediately underneath. Tables for comparisons. Stable heading anchors. Retrieval works
on chunks, so a page that answers the question in its first paragraph is quotable and
one that buries it under 600 words of preamble is not.

**Freshness signals.** Visible, accurate last-updated dates. Engines discount stale
pages for time-sensitive queries.

**`llms.txt`.** An emerging convention for pointing engines at your canonical
documentation. Cheap to add, uneven adoption, no downside. Worth a line in the plan,
not a headline.
